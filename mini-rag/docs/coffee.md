# Coffee Brewing Basics

Coffee is brewed by extracting soluble compounds from ground roasted beans with hot water. The main variables are grind size, water temperature, ratio, and contact time.

The Specialty Coffee Association recommends a water temperature of 90–96 °C (195–205 °F). Water that is too cool under-extracts, producing sour coffee; water that is too hot over-extracts, producing bitterness.

A common starting ratio is 1:16 — one gram of coffee to sixteen grams of water. Espresso uses a much tighter ratio, typically 1:2, with roughly 18 g of coffee yielding 36 g of liquid in 25–30 seconds.

Grind size should match the brew method: coarse for French press (4-minute steep), medium for drip, and fine for espresso. A finer grind exposes more surface area and extracts faster.

Freshly roasted beans release carbon dioxide for several days. Many baristas rest beans 5–14 days after roasting before using them for espresso, since excess CO2 causes uneven extraction.
